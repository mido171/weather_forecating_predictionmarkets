# V5+8 Model Description (KMIA, 06Z, V5_PLUS8_20260219T222321Z)

V5+8 is the strongest variant in the V5+ suite for KMIA daily Tmax at decision time 06:00 UTC on day T. It preserves the core E37 edge by keeping the online bias corrected MOS baseline and the onshore versus offshore mixture of experts, then adds a compact minute data bundle and a quantile based residual structure. The model is intentionally leak safe: it uses only MOS rows with asof_utc at or before 06Z and only minute observations at or before 06Z. The previous day is fully complete by that time, so full local day T minus 1 minute features are legal. V5+8 is a point forecast model that can also generate a residual distribution and calibrated probabilities for threshold events.

Decision time and leakage safety are non negotiable. The decision_utc(T) is fixed at T 06:00Z. MOS eligibility is enforced by asof_utc, so no MOS row issued after decision time can be used in any feature. For minute data, only ts_utc less than or equal to decision_utc is used. Full local day T minus 1 is allowed because that day is complete by 06Z. Day T minutes after 06Z are never used. The baseline bias correction uses truth only through T minus 1. These rules are the foundation of the model and ensure it can be used live without lookahead.

The baseline is feat_le_median_biascorr, the MOS ensemble median with an online bias correction state. This baseline is a strong point forecast by itself and is the reason E37 performs well. V5+8 does not attempt to replace it. Instead, it models residuals y(T) minus base(T) and adds a correction. This design is crucial because the residual is smaller and easier to learn, and because the baseline already captures most synoptic scale information.

The gate is a LightGBM classifier that predicts onshore probability using wind vector features and day of year seasonality. It takes feat_u, feat_v, feat_wsp_mean, cal_d_doy_sin, and cal_d_doy_cos. The gate output p_gate(T) is used to blend two residual experts. One expert is trained on onshore days, the other on offshore days. This soft mixture is more stable than a single global model because the error structure at KMIA differs strongly between marine flow and offshore flow.

The expert residual models are LightGBM regressors trained on a compact MOS feature set plus minute features and a small revision bundle. The MOS features include dewpoint depression proxies, temperature range signals, and cloud and precipitation predictors: feat_dd_models, feat_tmp_range_mean_models, feat_p12_max, feat_q12_max, feat_cig_min, plus wind vectors and seasonality. These are all available at or before the 06Z decision time through the asof rule.

The minute condensed features provide regime information that MOS alone does not capture. From local day T minus 1, the model uses iem_tmax, iem_tmin, the diurnal range, the timing of Tmax, a plateau duration near Tmax, mid day heating slopes, evening cooling slopes, and convective drop indicators such as max_drop_30 and drop_cnt_15_19. These features summarize whether the previous day was suppressed by clouds or convection, whether heating was delayed, and how sharp the afternoon cooling was. This is precisely the local physics that often drives MOS residuals.

From day T up to 06Z, the model uses T00, T03, and T06 temperature snapshots, the overnight cooling amount night_drop_00_06, a short window slope, and a short window variability. These features capture the current night airmass, stability, and cloud influence. They provide early morning information about the regime that will influence the next day Tmax. All of these are computed from minute data that is strictly within the 06Z cutoff.

A translator feature aligns the IEM minute observations with the NWS settlement sensor. The model tracks diff(T minus 1) = NWS Tmax minus IEM Tmax and uses EWMA smoothing to create diff_ewma_30 and diff_std_30. It also computes T06_adj by adding the EWMA difference to the raw 06Z temperature. This helps the model use minute observations without being misled by station differences or systematic offsets.

MOS revision dynamics provide a small but high value signal about rapidly changing forecasts. For a few key variables, V5+8 computes b0, b12, and b24 snapshots, where b0 is the latest eligible forecast at or before decision time and b12 and b24 are the latest eligible forecasts at or before decision time minus 12 or 24 hours. The model uses absolute revisions for temperature, moisture, and cloud fields, and cross model disagreement between GFS and NAM. These features are compact but effective in catching front timing and tropical cloud deck shifts that often cause large errors.

V5+8 extends the V5 residual correction with a quantile residual layer. Instead of predicting only a single residual, it trains quantile models at 0.1, 0.5, and 0.9 for the residual using the same expert feature set. The spread between r90 and r10 acts as a regime specific uncertainty signal. The final residual correction is r50 multiplied by a shrinkage weight w = exp(-k * spread), and the final point prediction is base + w * r50. The shrinkage factor k is selected by a small grid search on the validation set. In the current run, k equals 0.0, which means no shrinkage is applied, but the quantile models still provide a structured distribution for calibration and trading.

Training and evaluation use a fixed split. Training covers 2002 through 2019. Validation covers 2020 through 2022. Testing covers 2023 through 2025. The gate and expert models are trained with early stopping and a fixed seed for reproducibility. The quantile models are trained with LightGBM quantile objective and are evaluated using pinball loss and CRPS on validation and test. This split discipline avoids any use of future data when selecting parameters or assessing performance.

Performance is strong and stable. V5+8 achieved train MAE 0.7526, validation MAE 0.6954, and test MAE 0.6904 with test bias 0.0971. This is the best MAE among the V5+ variants. The improvement is modest compared to V5 in absolute terms, but it is consistent across the full test window, which signals a real structural gain rather than noise. The point forecast remains anchored by the strong baseline and is not destabilized by the new components.

The quantile residual models enable a distributional view. On validation and test, the quantile residual method achieved lower CRPS than a simple conformal residual distribution, making it the preferred calibration path for V5+8. The model can now provide estimated quantiles for Tmax and can compute probabilities for events such as Tmax greater than or equal to 85 or 90. This is useful for prediction markets, where expected value depends on probability estimates, not only point forecasts.

Operationally, V5+8 is designed to be self sufficient. It can rebuild its feature store from MOS and minute data, compute revision features from the mos_daily_value table, and produce predictions with a clear audit trail. The decision time is explicit. The MOS asof cutoff is explicit. The minute data window is explicit. This makes the model auditable and repeatable and allows it to be used in a live setting with strict leakage controls.

The strengths of V5+8 are clear. It keeps the winning E37 structure: a strong bias corrected MOS baseline plus regime specific residual correction. It adds high signal minute features that reflect local regime persistence and overnight airmass conditions. It adds MOS revision dynamics to capture rapid guidance changes. It also introduces a quantile residual structure that supports probability estimation and better calibrated decisions. Each component has a physical or statistical rationale, which makes the model robust and defensible.

There are also limitations. The model still shows a small positive bias and can be challenged by rare extreme events or abrupt regime shifts. The shrinkage weight is currently zero, which indicates the model did not find evidence that shrinking the residual improves MAE at this stage. That is not a failure, but it means the distributional layer is currently more valuable for calibration than for point forecast improvement. The model could still benefit from a regime specific bias offset or a more aggressive uncertainty aware shrinkage if future validation supports it.

Future improvements can be additive and safe. A simple regime calibrated bias adjustment could remove the remaining warm bias. A small second stage corrector that targets the worst outliers could reduce tail errors without destabilizing the mean. A richer quantile grid could improve CDF smoothness and probability estimates. These changes can be done while preserving the core baseline and gate structure, which is the heart of the E37 family.

In summary, V5+8 is a disciplined evolution of the E37 and V5 models. It preserves the strong bias corrected baseline, uses an onshore versus offshore mixture of experts, and adds minute condensed features and MOS revision dynamics to capture local regime information. The quantile residual layer provides a structured uncertainty estimate and enables calibrated probabilities. The model is leak safe by design, operationally reproducible, and well suited for live prediction market decisions at the 06Z decision time. It is ready for live deployment with clear audit trails, explicit data windows, and stable, repeatable predictions today safely.
