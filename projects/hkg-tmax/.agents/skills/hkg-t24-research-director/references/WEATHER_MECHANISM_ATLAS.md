# HKG Tmax Weather Mechanism Atlas

This is a hypothesis atlas. Every mechanism must be mapped to actual safe variables and tested.

## 1. Synoptic warm advection

Signals:

- regional temperature gradients;
- wind vector and persistence;
- pressure tendency;
- upwind station anomaly;
- lower-troposphere diagnostic warmth;
- forecast text.

Expected effects:

- rising target Tmax;
- possible official underforecast during rapid transitions.

Tests:

- wind-conditioned upwind anomalies;
- propagation;
- target slope interaction;
- source residual correction.

## 2. Cool surge onset and persistence

Signals:

- pressure rise;
- northerly/easterly flow;
- northern station cooling;
- dew-point drop;
- target downward slope;
- station rank changes.

Expected effects:

- suppressed Tmax;
- overforecast risk if surge strength is underestimated.

Separate onset, mature surge, and persistence.

## 3. Cool-surge breakdown and rebound

Signals:

- pressure tendency reversal;
- wind shift;
- inland/urban warming before HKO;
- target curvature turning positive;
- dew-point change;
- official forecast relative to recent cool state.

Expected effects:

- hot underforecast or rapid warming risk.

## 4. Marine suppression and sea-breeze penetration

Signals:

- coastal stations cooler than inland;
- onshore/easterly wind;
- rising dew point;
- small temperature-dew-point spread;
- coastal rank reversal;
- marine diagnostic data;
- pressure gradient.

Expected effects:

- HKO Tmax below inland heating potential;
- official overforecast when anchor overweights inland warmth.

## 5. Inland heating potential

Signals:

- inland station anomaly;
- weak wind;
- dry-air spread;
- stable/rising pressure in warm season;
- target hot-spell state;
- urban/open exposure contrast.

Expected effects:

- higher Tmax if marine penetration is weak;
- hot underforecast risk.

## 6. Humid cloudy suppression

Signals:

- dew-point surge;
- high moisture anomaly;
- low visibility/haze;
- rain/cloud forecast text;
- small temperature-dew-point spread;
- pressure fall;
- station-network weak heating.

Expected effects:

- reduced sensible heating and overforecast risk.

High dew point alone is ambiguous; condition on wind, cloud/rain proxies, and station warming.

## 7. Cloud-break warming

Signals:

- prior humid/cloudy state;
- falling moisture or clearing text;
- rapid morning warming in recent live feeds;
- solar/UV increase;
- station rank convergence;
- pressure change.

Expected effects:

- sudden underforecast as suppression ends.

## 8. Subtropical ridge and subsidence

Signals:

- upper-air height diagnostic;
- surface pressure/ridge proxy;
- dry-air spread;
- weak winds;
- broad station warmth;
- clear/sunny text;
- low station disagreement.

Expected effects:

- strong heating, especially when moisture/cloud suppression is absent.

Develop safe proxies for blocked upper-air fields.

## 9. Boundary-layer mixing

Signals:

- temperature-dew-point spread;
- wind speed;
- pressure/ridge state;
- morning heating rate;
- vertical diagnostic lapse rate;
- station convergence/divergence.

Expected effects:

- deeper mixing can raise or cap surface Tmax depending on aloft temperature and marine air.

## 10. Urban heat storage and nocturnal memory

Signals:

- recent HKO Tmax and Tmin where safe;
- multi-day hot spell;
- urban versus open station anomaly;
- weak overnight ventilation;
- target volatility;
- cooling-rate proxy.

Expected effects:

- persistence and reduced mean reversion during heat spells.

## 11. Rain-cooled and wet-surface suppression

Signals:

- antecedent rain;
- wet spell;
- humidity;
- cloud text;
- reduced station warming;
- lower visibility;
- lightning/radar prospective data.

Expected effects:

- lower Tmax and increased uncertainty.

Daily rainfall publication timing must be proven or lagged safely.

## 12. Dry-air heating

Signals:

- falling dew point;
- large temperature-dew-point spread;
- offshore/northerly/westerly flow depending on season;
- sunshine/clear text;
- pressure state.

Expected effects:

- stronger diurnal temperature rise, but winter air mass may still be cool. Condition on target level and season.

## 13. Weak-wind stagnation heat buildup

Signals:

- low vector wind;
- persistent warm station anomalies;
- target hot spell;
- pressure stability;
- urban-open contrast.

Expected effects:

- heat accumulation and underforecast risk.

## 14. Strong-wind mixing or marine ventilation

Signals:

- wind speed and direction;
- coastal/inland spread;
- station temperature convergence;
- moisture state.

Expected effects:

- direction-dependent suppression or mixing.

## 15. Tropical-cyclone influence

Possible states:

- pre-cyclone subsidence and heat;
- cloud shield and rain suppression;
- strong onshore marine flow;
- post-cyclone clearing;
- quadrant-dependent wind and moisture.

Use best track diagnostically unless operational advisory vintage is available. Safe proxies may come from pressure, wind, forecast text, and station gradients.

## 16. MAM transition

Combine:

- cool/warm target momentum;
- moisture surge;
- pressure transition;
- wind shift;
- coastal-inland divergence;
- low visibility/cloud proxies;
- official deviation from recent target.

Use soft states: cool humid, cool dry, warming break, marine suppression, post-front rebound.

## 17. JJA humid ceiling versus subsidence spike

Separate:

- persistent humid heat with limited diurnal range;
- cloud/rain-suppressed days;
- weak-wind urban heat;
- dry/subsident spikes;
- sea-breeze suppression.

## 18. SON drying and typhoon transition

Signals:

- dew-point decline;
- pressure rise;
- clearer text;
- residual cyclone influence;
- changing wind sectors;
- persistent summer heat decay.

## 19. DJF monsoon modulation

Separate:

- surge onset;
- mature cool regime;
- clear-dry daytime rebound;
- moderation and breakdown;
- humid easterly episodes.

## 20. Forecast-source and human-adjustment regimes

The official forecast may incorporate mechanisms not visible in local data. Source changes, forecast range, text, and revisions can reveal confidence or emerging regime recognition. Test where station/target signals contradict the anchor and whether the anchor or correction wins.

## Mechanism experiment requirements

For each mechanism:

- operational trigger;
- diagnostic evidence;
- safe features;
- expected residual sign;
- season;
- minimum support;
- rival explanation;
- ablations;
- no-harm gate;
- proxy plan for blocked variables.
